# Bio

I'm an Associate Professor of Computer Science at Nanjing University, where I also received my B.S. in 2011 and Ph.D. in 2017 under the advice of Prof. Chang Xu, Xiaoxing Ma, and Jian Lyu. In 2015, I spent a memorable half-year at The Ohio State University as a visiting Ph.D. student, working with Professor Feng Qin—and, for a while, happily becoming a Buckeye.

I'm interested in *agentic AI systems and the technologies behind test-time scaling*, with general intelligence as the broader horizon. Before agents and language models, I worked on systems software and software automation: operating system kernels, compilers, virtual machines, middleware, and the machinery through which software constructs and transforms itself.

In Fall 2026, I will launch *Generative Software Engineering*, to pursuit the understanding of how software is built, and then rebuilding the way we build it. I'll discontinue the *Operating Systems* and Project-N (proudly with Zihao Yu), which grew beyond the classroom: the lectures have received more than 5,000,000 views, supported by the *Hitchhiker’s Guide to Operating Systems* paper.


Competitive programming was once a part of my life. I won first prizes in regional contests, placed second in the 2012 Tencent Hackathon, and competed in the 2009 ACM-ICPC World Finals. I later spent 20 years setting problems for the Jiangsu Provincial Programming Competition, eventually serving as chair of its technical committee, and served for three years on the committee of the Asia-Pacific Informatics Olympiad. Now that that chapter is also coming to an end. I'm retiring from the competition committee: it's time to move on.
